# How city bulk pickup works in the U.S.

Getting rid of a mattress or sofa is rarely “just put it in the trash.” Most U.S. cities run a **bulky / large-item / heavy trash** program with one of three models. Knowing which model your city uses saves a missed pickup — or a fine.

## 1. Scheduled zone or route day

Your neighborhood gets bulky service on a set week or day (sometimes monthly, sometimes a few times a year). You set items at the curb in a window the city publishes. Examples in our coverage include parts of Houston’s heavy trash system and Tucson’s Brush and Bulky sweeps.

**What to check:** set-out day, cubic-yard limits, and whether Freon appliances need a separate call.

## 2. Appointment / on-request pickup

You book through 311, an online portal, or the utility account. Caps often look like “up to N items” or “one truckload.” Kansas City, Atlanta (city limits), Tulsa, and Louisville’s Urban Services District use appointment-style bulk.

**What to check:** free allotment per year, cancellation deadlines, and whether tires or TVs count toward the limit.

## 3. Drop-off first (little or no free curb bulk)

Some cities push residents to citizen collection stations or convenience centers, with fee-based special pickup as a backup. El Paso’s Citizen Collection Stations are a clear example.

**What to check:** ID / water-bill proof, hours, and what is banned at the gate (tires and Freon rules vary).

## Freon, e-waste, and HHW are different channels

Even when furniture rides with bulk, **refrigerators, TVs, paint, and batteries** often do not. Cities split these into HHW facilities, e-waste events, or licensed recyclers. Our dispose pages call that out per city instead of pretending one curb pile fits everything.

## Use a city guide, then a center

1. Open your [verified city](/cities) and the item you need (start with [mattress](/materials/mattress) or [refrigerator](/materials/refrigerator)).
2. Follow the official program link on that page.
3. If the answer is drop-off, use [Centers](/centers) to find a verified site near your ZIP.

DumpRegistry only publishes city-sourced program paths with a named source and verification date — not statewide filler labeled as local advice.
