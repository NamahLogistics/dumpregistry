# How to dispose of e-waste in the U.S.

E-waste is electronics: TVs, computers, monitors, printers, phones, tablets, and mixed peripherals. Many states and cities **ban** these from landfill carts. Even where a ban is soft, blue bins are the wrong place — circuit boards and CRTs break and contaminate paper and plastic streams.

## What counts as e-waste

- [Televisions](/materials/television) and [computer monitors](/materials/computer-monitor) (CRTs are the hardest)
- [Laptops](/materials/laptop), [desktops](/materials/desktop-computer), [tablets](/materials/tablet), [smartphones](/materials/smartphone)
- [Printers](/materials/printer) and peripherals
- Mixed boxes of cables and small electronics — see [mixed e-waste](/materials/e-waste-mixed)

Appliances with Freon (fridges, ACs) are usually **not** standard e-waste — see [Freon appliances](/guides/freon-appliance-disposal).

## Data security before you recycle

- Sign out of accounts; remove SIM and memory cards
- Factory-reset phones and tablets
- Wipe or remove [hard drives](/materials/hard-drive) from PCs if you need stronger assurance
- Shred or physically destroy drives when policy requires it

A recycler is not your backup plan for uncleared cloud sessions — do that first.

## Where e-waste actually goes

### Municipal e-waste drop-off or events

Cities publish free or fee-based electronics days. CRTs sometimes cost extra or are refused. Bring ID if residency-limited.

### Retailer take-back

Electronics retailers and some carriers take phones, tablets, and select accessories. TV acceptance is narrower. Ask about manufacturer mail-back for printers and ink — see [ink / toner](/materials/ink-toner).

### Certified recyclers

Look for responsible recycling certifications when you have business volumes or old servers. Residential users can still ask municipal sites where material is processed.

## CRT TVs and monitors

Cathode-ray tubes are leaded glass. Many “free e-waste” events exclude them or charge. Do not abandon a CRT on the curb hoping bulk day covers it — check the accepted list.

## What not to do

- Put a TV in the blue cart
- Smash a CRT in the driveway
- Leave a pile of computers at a closed event gate
- Donate non-working electronics to thrift stores that only want tested goods
- Skip the wipe on a laptop that still powers on
