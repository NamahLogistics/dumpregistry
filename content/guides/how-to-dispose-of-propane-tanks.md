# How to dispose of propane tanks and cylinders

Propane tanks are pressurized containers. That alone is why almost every U.S. trash and recycling program bans them from carts and dumpsters. A tank in a garbage truck can become a projectile or fire hazard when compacted. Disposal is usually an **exchange**, **refill/return**, or **HHW / specialty** problem — not a bulky-furniture problem.

## Know which tank you have

### 20 lb BBQ / grill tanks

These are the common white or gray cylinders under gas grills. Best path for most households:

- **Exchange programs** at grocery, hardware, and big-big-box stores — swap empty for full
- **Refill** at propane dealers if your tank is still in certification date
- Return or recycle obsolete tanks through the exchange partner or a propane distributor

If the collar is damaged, the tank is out of date for refill, or the valve is bad, ask the exchange desk what they accept for scrap. Do not abandon tanks behind the store.

### 1 lb camping / torch cylinders

Disposable one-pound cylinders are harder. Many curbside programs reject them even when empty. Common legal paths:

- City or county **HHW** facilities that list propane cylinders
- Specialty camping or hardware take-back events (not universal)
- Some transfer stations with a segregated propane cage

Empty does not always mean “trash OK.” Assume you need a listed program unless your city guide explicitly says otherwise.

### Forklift / large ASME tanks

Those are commercial. Use the propane supplier that owns or services the tank. Residential guides on DumpRegistry focus on household cylinders.

## Why “just empty it and trash it” fails

People puncture tanks or vent them in the yard to “make them safe.” That is unsafe and often illegal. Risks include:

- Fire or explosion from residual vapor
- Injury from uncontrolled venting
- Fines when a scavenged tank shows up in a landfill load

Only follow manufacturer or program instructions for any venting step. If a site requires tanks to be empty, ask them how they define empty and whether they accept valves attached.

## Fire extinguishers and helium tanks are cousins, not twins

Pressurized containers share the “not in the cart” rule, but programs differ:

- [Fire extinguishers](/materials/fire-extinguisher) — often HHW or fire-department guidance
- [Helium tanks](/materials/helium-tank) — disposable party tanks may need specialty scrap rules printed on the label

Do not assume a propane exchange cage will take extinguishers or helium.

## Apartment and HOA realities

Many multifamily dumpsters explicitly ban propane. Leaving a tank beside the dumpster is still dumping. Use exchange on your next store run, or book an HHW trip. If you are moving out, tanks are easy to forget in a garage corner — put them on the checklist with paint and batteries.

## What not to do

- Put any propane cylinder in trash or recycling
- Throw a tank in a metal scrap dumpster without asking (many yards reject pressurized vessels)
- Store damaged, rusted-through tanks indoors near heaters
- Transport a tank loose in a passenger cabin — secure upright in a ventilated vehicle area
- Paint over or remove certification collar information before exchange
