# How to dispose of Styrofoam and packing foam

“Styrofoam recycling near me” usually fails because **expanded polystyrene (EPS)** is technically recyclable and practically rejected. It is mostly air. It shatters into beads that contaminate paper and containers at a materials recovery facility. U.S. EPA consumer guidance and nearly every large-city recycling page agree: **foam does not go in the blue cart.**

DSNY’s own recycling list names foam products (including packing peanuts) among items that are **not** NYC metal/glass/plastic recycling. The city’s 2017 foam determination went further: food-service EPS was found not recyclable in an economically feasible way for New York, which is why NYC restricted food-service foam and the *sale* of packing peanuts — leftover household foam still goes in **garbage**, not recycling.

This guide is about **what you already have**. It is not a map of secret densifiers we did not verify.

## Three different foams (do not mix them)

### 1. White block / “TV box” EPS

Rigid white foam that protected an appliance or TV. This is the grade specialty **densifiers** want when a drop-off exists: clean, dry, tape and film peeled off, usually **white only**. Colored foam and foam that squishes back like a gym mat is often a different plastic.

### 2. Packing peanuts

Loose-fill. Some shipping stores **reuse** clean peanuts. That is reuse, not recycling. Starch / “biodegradable” peanuts look similar and can ruin an EPS bale — they dissolve in water; EPS does not. Grocery **film/bag** bins do not want peanuts.

### 3. Food trays, cups, clamshells

Meat trays, takeout boxes, foam cups. Usually **trash** even at foam drop-offs because of food and fat. Several states and cities restrict *selling* EPS foodware; that does not magically create a recycling stream for the tray in your fridge.

Flexible foam (couch cushions, pool noodles, foam board insulation) is **not** EPS drop-off material.

## Path A: Specialty EPS drop-off / densifier

A handful of municipal environmental centers and private recyclers run a densifier: they shred and compact clean EPS into dense logs that can be shipped. Frisco, Texas publishes a clear resident example (clean packaging foam; meat trays never; starch peanuts no).

Rules that show up wherever these sites exist:

- Clean and dry; no tape, cardboard, or plastic wrap
- White rigid packaging foam — not dirty foodware unless that site says otherwise
- Call before you drive; many sites are self-serve with odd hours
- Do not put foam in the same gaylord as film bags

DumpRegistry **does not invent** a densifier for your ZIP. If your [city styrofoam page](/materials/styrofoam) names a real facility, use it. If it does not, there probably is not a city-run one.

## Path B: Packing-peanut reuse

Clean, dry EPS peanuts can sometimes go back to a parcel store that still uses them. Ask. They will refuse wet, dirty, or starch peanuts. This does nothing for block foam or food trays.

## Path C: Trash (the honest default)

When there is no densifier and no reuse:

- Bag foam so beads do not blow down the street
- Put it in **garbage**, not recycling
- Do not burn it

That is what NYC Sanitation tells residents. It is also what happens to foam that is wishcycled into a blue cart — it is sorted out and landfilled after contaminating the load.

## Do not use the grocery bag bin

Most U.S. cities send **plastic film** (bags, wrap) to store take-back because film tangles sorting screens. Our city styrofoam pages often **share that film pathway in data** because both are “not curbside recycling.” That does **not** mean a supermarket bag barrel wants a microwave-sized foam block.

- Film / bags → store drop-off when the city says so ([plastic bags](/materials/plastic-bags))
- EPS blocks → densifier or trash
- Peanuts → reuse at a shipper, or trash

If a clerk says “we don’t take Styrofoam,” believe them.

## Prep if you did find a densifier

- Break blocks down so they fit the hopper; do not grind them into snow in your driveway
- Peel stickers and film
- Keep it dry (wet foam is a rejection)
- Separate peanuts from blocks unless the site says to mix

## What not to do

- Blue-bin foam “because it has a 6”
- Stuff foam into a cardboard box and hope the MRF sorts it
- Mix meat trays into a clean-packaging drop-off
- Dump beads in a storm drain or vacant lot
- Confuse foam with [cardboard](/materials/cardboard) or [plastic bags](/materials/plastic-bags)
