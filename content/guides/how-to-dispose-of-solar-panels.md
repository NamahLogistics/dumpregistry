# How to dispose of solar panels

“Recycle solar panels near me” is already showing up in DumpRegistry Search Console. The miss on our city pages was treating a **photovoltaic module** as mixed household e-waste. It is not. A TV/computer drop-off is built for screens and towers. A panel is glass, polymer laminate, a junction box, and (often) an aluminum frame — and it **still generates voltage in daylight**.

This guide is the national rule. City pages should confirm solar / PV on the official program, not copy the e-waste answer.

## What you actually have

### Intact rooftop or ground-mount modules

Full-size panels from a home array. These are the ones municipal e-waste sites refuse. The installer who pulled them, or a specialty PV recycler, is the first call — not the Saturday electronics event.

### Broken or storm-damaged panels

Same product, sharper edges, still live in light. Cover the face. Do not stack cracked glass in a recycling cart. Do not leave shards in a driveway pile for bulky furniture pickup.

### Small off-grid / patio kits

Folding suitcase panels and 12-volt kits are still PV, not “small electronics.” Some retailers take them; many city e-waste lines do not. Ask before you mix them into a TV load.

### Inverters, batteries, and racking

Those are **different streams**. Hybrid/lithium storage follows [lithium battery](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-lithium-batteries) rules. Lead-acid follows [car battery](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-a-car-battery). Steel racking is scrap metal. Do not bury those inside a “solar” drop-off question.

## Why mixed e-waste is the wrong clone

Household e-waste programs (S.A.F.E. Centers, DSNY Special Waste electronics, Houston ESCs, Chicago HCCRF, Miramar HHW) publish lists like TVs, computers, printers, and phones. A PV module is usually **absent**. Hauling a panel there wastes a trip and can get you turned away at the gate.

DumpRegistry city solar pages must say that. They should cite the official city/county source and tell you to **confirm solar / PV** — not “same channel as mixed e-waste.”

## Path A: Installer or developer take-back

If a contractor is removing the array, disposal is part of the job in many contracts. Ask who the recycler is. A cheap “we’ll take it” with no destination is how panels get dumped.

## Path B: Specialty PV recycler or transfer / C&D

Some transfer stations and C&D sites take modules as construction-related material, often with a fee. Specialty PV recyclers recover glass, aluminum, and (in some plants) silver and silicon. Call first. Do not invent a densifier or a free municipal PV program that the city page does not name.

## Path C: Manufacturer or state program

A few states and manufacturers run take-back or stewardship for PV. That is **not** national. If your city page does not name one, do not assume California or New York electronics law covers rooftop modules.

## How to handle a panel

- Work in daylight with the glass **covered** (cardboard or a blanket) so it is not an open circuit in the sun
- Tape or cap leads; do not carry a panel by the cable
- Do not throw it in a roll-off with mixed C&D unless that site listed PV
- Do not put it on bulky furniture day next to a sofa

## What not to do

- City e-waste as the default
- Trash or recycling cart
- Leaving a cracked panel uncovered in a yard
- Mixing lithium storage batteries into the same load without saying so
- Following a [mixed e-waste](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-ewaste) page as if it were solar
