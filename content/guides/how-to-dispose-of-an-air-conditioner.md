# How to dispose of an air conditioner (window or central)

Air conditioners are not “just another heavy box.” Most residential units contain **refrigerant** that is illegal to vent under U.S. environmental rules. Cities therefore route ACs through appliance / Freon programs, certified haulers, or drop-off sites that can handle sealed systems — not through the same channel as a sofa.

For the broader Freon pattern across fridges and freezers, see [Freon appliance disposal](/guides/freon-appliance-disposal). This guide focuses on air conditioners.

## Window units vs central systems

### Window and portable ACs

These are the units homeowners most often need to dispose of after a move or replacement. Typical paths:

- City **appliance / Freon** bulky appointment
- Utility or energy-efficiency **haul-away / rebate** programs (seasonal)
- Retailer take-back when you buy a new unit
- Transfer station or recycler that accepts Freon appliances (fee common)
- Licensed appliance hauler

### Central air condensers and air handlers

Outdoor condensers are contractor territory for most households. A HVAC company replacing the system should recover refrigerant and haul the old condenser. If you inherited a dead unit on a slab, hire a Freon-certified remover — do not cut copper lines to “make it lighter.”

## Why HHW is usually the wrong first stop

Household hazardous waste buildings love paint and batteries. They often **reject** Freon appliances. Starting at HHW wastes a trip unless your city guide explicitly lists ACs there.

## Prep before pickup or drop-off

- Unplug and drain any window-unit water
- Remove the unit safely from the sash — two people for heavier models
- Do not puncture coils or cut lines
- Check whether your city wants cords secured or doors/covers handled a certain way
- Ask about stairs: many municipal crews will not carry a unit down multiple flights

Portable ACs with exhaust hoses still follow Freon rules if they contain refrigerant.

## Dehumidifiers and ACs get mixed up

Many dehumidifiers also contain refrigerant and follow similar rules. See [dehumidifier](/materials/dehumidifier). Microwaves and washers usually do **not** share the Freon path — do not put them on the same special load unless the city says appliances are grouped.

## Illegal dumping and Freon myths

Common bad advice online:

- “Bleed the Freon and scrap the metal” — venting is illegal without certified recovery
- “Put it out with bulk trash like a dresser” — crews may leave it and ticket you
- “Scrap yards always take them” — many reject Freon units without paperwork

If a quote seems too cheap, ask whether refrigerant recovery is included.

## What not to do

- Leave a window unit on the sidewalk indefinitely after move-out
- Smash the compressor to “drain it”
- Mix an AC into a construction debris roll-off without telling the hauler
- Assume apartment maintenance will take a personal unit for free
- Store a damaged unit that is actively leaking oily residue indoors without ventilation and a professional plan
