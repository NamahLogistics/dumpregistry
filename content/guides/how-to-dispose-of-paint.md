# How to dispose of paint (latex and oil-based)

Paint is one of the most searched household disposal problems in the U.S. The answer depends on chemistry: **latex (water-based)** and **oil-based** are not the same stream. Getting that wrong is how people get rejected at the gate or create a hazardous load in a trash truck.

## Step 1: Read the can

- **Latex / acrylic / water-based** — often solidifiable or PaintCare/HHW
- **Oil-based / alkyd / stain / solvent** — almost always household hazardous waste (HHW)
- **Aerosol spray paint** — usually HHW; do not puncture

If the label is gone, treat unknown liquid paint as HHW.

## Latex paint: what usually works

Many cities allow **fully dried** latex to be trashed as a solid. That means:

- Absorb leftover liquid with cat litter, sawdust, or a commercial hardener
- Leave the lid off only if your city instructs that method
- Put dried cans in the trash if local rules allow — empty metal cans sometimes recycle when bone-dry and accepted

Never pour latex down a drain or storm sewer. “Water-based” does not mean sewer-safe in volume.

Some states participate in **PaintCare** or similar producer programs — drop-off at paint retailers. That is often easier than county HHW for latex and many oil products. Confirm your site accepts the product type before you drive.

See [latex paint](/materials/paint-latex).

## Oil-based paint: HHW only

Oil-based paint, spar varnish, and many primers are flammable HHW. Do not dry them in the open as a DIY trash hack if your city requires HHW intake. Transport upright, sealed, and labeled. See [oil-based paint](/materials/paint-oil).

## Partial cans and “leave it for the next owner”

Leaving usable paint for a neighbor is fine if you arrange pickup. Abandoning open cans in an alley is dumping. For move-outs, budget an HHW trip the same week you handle batteries and bulbs.

## What not to do

- Mix oil and latex in one jug “to finish it”
- Hide liquid paint in a black trash bag
- Toss aerosol cans in recycling full of product
- Burn paint cans
- Assume every hardware store is a PaintCare site
