# How to dispose of construction debris from a home project

Remodeling waste is not household garbage. Boards, drywall, tile, concrete chunks, old cabinets, and roofing tear-off are **construction and demolition (C&D)** materials. Stuffing them into a residential cart is the fastest way to get a rejection tag, a fine, or a surprise bill from your hauler.

This guide is for homeowners and small DIY jobs. Licensed contractors usually already have C&D accounts and roll-offs — if you hired one, ask who owns the debris before you start self-hauling.

## What counts as construction debris

Typical C&D from a home project:

- Dimensional lumber, plywood, and trim
- Drywall and plaster
- Concrete, brick, and block
- Asphalt shingles
- Old doors, cabinets, and vanity carcasses
- Flooring tear-out (not always recyclable)

Related but often separate streams:

- [Yard waste](/materials/yard-waste) from landscaping — usually organics, not C&D
- Paint, solvents, and adhesives — often [HHW](/guides/hhw-vs-bulk-vs-ewaste), not C&D
- Appliances pulled during a remodel — follow appliance / Freon rules, not the debris pile

## Path A: Roll-off dumpster at the house

For kitchen gut jobs, deck tear-offs, or flooring replacement, a roll-off is usually cheapest in time even if not cheapest in tip fees.

- Size for the job; overfilling with dirt makes the haul overweight
- Ask what is prohibited (tires, batteries, paint cans, Freon appliances)
- Keep the driveway protected; get HOA approval if required
- Do not let neighbors add mystery junk — you pay for their mattress

## Path B: Self-haul to a C&D or transfer station

If you have a truck:

- Confirm the site accepts residential C&D (some are commercial-only)
- Bring payment and ID
- Expect fees by ton; wet drywall and concrete are heavy
- Separate clean loads when the gate price rewards it (clean concrete vs mixed debris)

## Path C: City bulky for a few pieces only

Some cities allow a limited number of remodeling leftovers on bulky day — a door, a vanity, a short stack of boards. That is not a full kitchen demo. If the pile looks like a contractor load, expect refusal. Check your city bulky rules before you build a sidewalk mountain.

## Separate when it saves money

### Concrete

Clean concrete is often recyclable as aggregate. Rebar and heavy soil contamination change the price. See [concrete](/materials/concrete).

### Drywall

Clean new scrap sometimes recycles; painted demolition drywall often does not. Pre-1980s materials can raise asbestos questions — stop and test if you are unsure. See [drywall](/materials/drywall).

### Lumber

Untreated wood may go to a wood recycler; treated lumber is restricted. Pull out what is reusable before you pay to tip it. See [lumber / wood](/materials/lumber).

### Asphalt shingles

Dense and often a specialty C&D stream. See [asphalt shingles](/materials/asphalt-shingles).

## Hazardous materials hiding in “debris”

Pull these out of the roll-off before the truck arrives:

- Oil-based paint and stain — [oil-based paint](/materials/paint-oil)
- Solvents, adhesives, and roofing cement liquids
- Thermostats or thermometers with mercury
- Fluorescent tubes from old fixtures — [fluorescent bulbs](/materials/fluorescent-bulbs)
- Batteries from demo’d devices

Mixed hazardous waste in a C&D load can get the entire container rejected.

## What not to do

- Bury debris in the backyard
- Burn treated wood or asphalt shingles
- Fill a rented apartment dumpster with demo waste without written permission
- Stack debris in the street overnight where it blocks drainage or sidewalks
- Assume “the city will take it” without reading bulky vs C&D rules
