# How to dispose of yard waste the right way

Yard waste is leaves, grass clippings, brush, branches, and garden trimmings. In cities with organics programs it is a resource. In cities without, it is still usually banned from the landfill cart as loose debris or limited to bundled brush rules. Either way, treating a pile of branches like ordinary trash creates missed pickups and contamination fees.

## Know your city’s organics model

U.S. cities generally fall into a few patterns:

- **Weekly green / organics cart** — Yard waste (and sometimes food scraps) in a designated cart
- **Yard-waste-only season** — Spring through fall collection; winter may pause
- **Bagged leaf programs** — Paper bags only; plastic bags rejected
- **Brush bundling rules** — Tied bundles with length/diameter caps on bulky or yard days
- **Drop-off mulch sites** — Self-haul to a city compost or yard-waste facility

DumpRegistry city guides spell out which pattern applies where we have verified sources. National blogs that say “just compost everything” skip HOA rules, apartment limits, and contamination lists.

## What usually belongs in yard waste

- Leaves and pine needles
- Grass clippings (unless your city asks you to grasscycle)
- Small prunings and brush within size limits
- Untreated wood chips from your own property (sometimes)

## What usually contaminates the load

These turn a good organics load into trash or a rejected cart:

- Plastic bags, trays, and plant pots
- Soil, rock, and concrete
- Treated lumber and painted wood
- Pet waste (often banned from green carts)
- Food scraps in cities that are yard-only (food belongs only where the program allows — see [food scraps](/materials/food-scraps))
- Flocked or decorated [Christmas trees](/materials/christmas-tree) with tinsel still attached

If your cart is tagged for contamination, fix the load before the next service day. Repeat tags can become fees.

## Branches and storm debris

After storms, cities sometimes add temporary brush collection. That is not a permanent invitation to stack contractor-sized wood piles. Follow published diameter and length limits. For large trunks, you may need a private chipper service or a transfer station.

## Christmas trees

Trees are seasonal yard waste. Remove stands, lights, ornaments, and tinsel. Flocked trees are frequently rejected from mulching. Use the city’s published pickup week — trees left out in February often become illegal dumping complaints. Details live on the [Christmas tree material hub](/materials/christmas-tree).

## Composting at home vs city collection

Backyard composting is great for leaves and non-meat scraps when you can manage pests and HOA rules. It does not replace city rules for large brush. Do not dump unfinished compost or invasive weeds where your city bans them from green bins.

## What not to do

- Blow leaves into the street and assume sweeping crews want them
- Bag yard waste in black plastic trash bags for the green cart
- Mix remodeling lumber into “yard” piles
- Dump brush in parks or vacant lots
- Overlook fire-season restrictions on green-waste drop-off sites in drought regions
