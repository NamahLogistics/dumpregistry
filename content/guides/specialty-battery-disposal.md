# Specialty and rechargeable battery disposal

Some Search Console queries are oddly specific (for example model-like “DDR battery disposal”). Most of the time that still means: **this is not an alkaline AA**, retail drop boxes may refuse it, and you need HHW or a specialty rechargeable program. Brand, model, or “DDR” strings on the label rarely change the disposal rule — chemistry and form factor do.

## Sort the chemistry

- **Alkaline household** — rules vary; many cities allow trash, some want HHW — [household batteries](/materials/household-batteries)
- **Lithium-ion** (phones, tools, scooters, laptop packs) — never trash — [lithium guide](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-lithium-batteries)
- **Lead-acid auto** — retailer core exchange — [car battery guide](/guides/how-to-dispose-of-a-car-battery)
- **NiMH / NiCd tool and camera packs** — usually HHW or brand take-back, not blue bin
- **Button / coin cells** — HHW or retail button-cell programs; tape both sides

If you cannot read the chemistry on the pack, treat it as HHW and bring a photo of the label.

## Prep before you leave home

- Tape exposed terminals with clear packing tape
- Keep packs dry and out of a hot car
- Bag swollen, leaking, or recalled cells separately and tell staff at the gate
- Do not toss packs into a mixed e-waste box unless the site says batteries are OK in that stream

## When the store bin rejects you

Retail battery buckets often accept only certain sizes (AA/AAA/9V) and refuse tool packs, scooter batteries, and anything damaged. Take rejected packs to:

- City or county HHW
- Brand / tool-manufacturer pack recycling
- E-waste events that explicitly list batteries
- DumpRegistry [Centers](/centers?material=lithium-battery) filtered to battery-capable sites in your ZIP

Ask which stream they want — some sites separate lithium from lead-acid and alkalines.

## Damaged or recalled packs

Do not charge them. Keep them cool and isolated in a non-flammable container and follow HHW guidance for damaged lithium. Smoking, sparking, or hot packs are an emergency-response issue, not a recycling errand — get people and pets away and call local emergency guidance if the pack is actively failing.
