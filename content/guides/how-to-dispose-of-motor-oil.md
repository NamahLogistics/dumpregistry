# How to recycle used motor oil (and filters)

Used motor oil is one of the few household hazardous wastes with a mature U.S. take-back system. EPA’s used-oil rules exist so do-it-yourself oil changes can be **collected and re-refined** instead of dumped. One oil change poured on the ground or into a storm drain can contaminate a huge volume of water — EPA’s long-running “You dump it, you drink it” campaign exists for that reason.

This guide is for **household DIY** oil (cars, trucks, motorcycles, mowers, small engines). A shop that changes oil for customers is a used-oil generator under different paperwork. DumpRegistry city pages are residential.

## What counts as used oil

Sites that accept “used oil” often also take related petroleum lubricants if they are **unmixed**:

- Crankcase motor oil
- Transmission fluid
- Gear oil and differential oil
- Hydraulic oil and power steering fluid (ask — not every counter takes these)

They usually **do not** take the same jug if you added:

- [Antifreeze / coolant](/materials/antifreeze)
- Gasoline, diesel, or brake fluid
- Paint, solvents, pesticides, or degreasers
- Water, dirt, or unknown garage leftovers

EPA’s used-oil guidance is blunt: **do not mix used oil with anything**. Mixed oil may be rejected, billed as hazardous waste, or illegal to pour into a collection tank. If you already mixed it, say so at the gate — do not hide it in a “motor oil” jug.

Cooking grease is a different stream. See [cooking oil](/guides/how-to-recycle-cooking-oil).

## Path A: Auto-parts store, lube shop, or certified collection center

This is the default for most DIY gallons.

- Many auto-parts retailers and quick-lube shops accept household used oil (and often filters)
- There is **no federal rule** that every store that *sells* oil must *take* used oil — call the location
- California runs CalRecycle **Certified Collection Centers** (typically parts stores and service stations) with posted DIY limits; other states use similar “oil drop” collection without the same branding
- Look for the petroleum-industry oil-drop symbol where it is still posted

Bring oil in a **clean, leak-proof container with a screw lid**. EPA consumer guidance: reuse the original oil bottles when you can. Do not use bleach jugs, pesticide bottles, or food containers.

Ask the counter about **volume limits** before you load five cases. City HHW pages we verified also post per-visit caps — for example NYC DSNY Special Waste lists **up to 10 quarts of motor oil/transmission fluid per visit** and **up to two oil filters**.

## Path B: City or county HHW

When the parts store is closed, full, or refuses mixed/unknown oil, use the hazardous-waste program on your city page:

- [Chicago](/illinois/chicago/dispose/motor-oil) — HCCRF (1150 N. Branch) lists auto fluids among household chemicals
- [New York](/new-york/new-york/dispose/motor-oil) — DSNY Special Waste Drop-Off (borough sites; oil/filter limits above)
- [Los Angeles](/california/los-angeles/dispose/motor-oil) — LASAN S.A.F.E. Centers (weekend HHW)
- [Houston](/texas/houston/dispose/motor-oil) — Environmental Service Centers, not carts
- [San Diego](/california/san-diego/dispose/motor-oil) — Miramar HHW Transfer Facility **by appointment** (used oil and filters listed)
- [Dallas](/texas/dallas/dispose/motor-oil) — Home Chemical Collection Center (automobile fluids / oil filters)

Some cities (Phoenix is one we verified) offer **HHW home collection** on a limited annual slot instead of a depot. That is still not “leave oil at the curb on trash day.”

## Path C: Curbside used-oil pickup (only where the hauler says so)

A minority of U.S. cities and counties let residents set out sealed oil jugs and bagged filters on a recycling or bulky schedule. That is a **named program**, not a default. If your DumpRegistry city page does not say curbside oil, do not invent it.

## Oil filters

A used filter can hold a large fraction of a quart. EPA and state used-oil-filter rules expect you to **drain free-flowing oil** (invert the filter over a pan; many household guides say overnight / 12–24 hours). Then:

- Bag the drained metal filter if the collection site asks for that
- Take undrained filters only where HHW or a collection center says they will accept them
- Cartridge (paper) filters are not the same as metal spin-on filters — ask

Empty plastic oil bottles that held **only** oil are often recyclable as plastic once drained; that is still not permission to toss a full filter in the blue cart.

## How to transport it

- Keep jugs upright in a tub or tray in the trunk
- Tighten caps; wipe the outside
- Do not leave oil in a hot car for days
- Keep it away from kids and pets — used oil contains metals and combustion residues

If a collection tank looks dirty or the attendant will not confirm they take DIY oil, use the official HHW site on your city page instead.

## What not to do

- Pour oil on the ground, into a ditch, or down a storm drain
- Dump it in household trash, a dumpster, or a recycling cart
- Mix it with [antifreeze](/materials/antifreeze), gasoline, or solvents “to get rid of both”
- Burn it in a backyard heater that is not a legal used-oil space heater (those rules are for specific equipment, not a fire pit)
- Pay a cash hauler who will not name the licensed used-oil receiver
