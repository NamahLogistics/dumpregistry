# How to dispose of a mattress in the U.S.

A mattress almost never belongs in the regular trash cart. Across U.S. cities it is treated as **bulky / large-item** waste, sometimes as a specialty mattress-recycling stream, and almost never as recycling. If you leave it out on a normal trash day, crews usually skip it — or you get a tag and a fine.

This guide covers the patterns you will see nationwide, what gets pickups rejected, and how to open a verified city program on DumpRegistry.

## Why mattresses are “special”

Mattresses are bulky, absorb moisture, and can harbor bed bugs. Cities respond with:

- Appointment or zone-day bulky collection
- Per-item caps (often one free mattress per year)
- Drop-off at a transfer station or mattress recycler
- Wrapping rules in some pest-sensitive metros

A box spring is usually a second bulky item. Check whether your city counts them separately toward the limit — see also [box spring](/materials/box-spring).

## Common disposal paths

### City bulky or large-item pickup

Most homeowners start here.

- **Zone day** — Your neighborhood has a published bulky window. Set out only then.
- **Appointment** — Book through 311, a utility portal, or a bulky request form.
- **Set-out rules** — Keep clear of cars and hydrants; some cities ban mattresses from alleys.

If the mattress is soaked or infested, free pickup may be denied. Ask before you drag it outside.

### Citizen drop-off

If you have a truck, drop-off is often faster than waiting weeks for bulky day. Expect residency ID and a tip fee. Some metros run mattress-only recycling events — those are seasonal; do not assume year-round.

### Donation (rare for mattresses)

Many charities no longer accept used mattresses. Call first. Hotels and dorm surplus sometimes go through commercial recyclers, not thrift stores.

### Paid hauler or retailer haul-away

Buying a new mattress sometimes includes haul-away of one old unit — confirm before delivery. For move-outs, a licensed junk hauler beats anonymous curb dumping. Ask where the load goes.

## Bed bugs, wrapping, and neighbors

Some cities require plastic wrapping; others ban wrapping because crews cannot check for pests. Follow the city page, not a viral tip. Leaving a mattress on a shared porch or hallway without building approval is how disputes and tickets start.

## What not to do

- Stuff a mattress into a dumpster that is not rated for bulky waste
- Dump it in a vacant lot or park (illegal dumping)
- Cut it open to “make recycling” — most MRFs reject shredded mattresses from residents
- Assume the HOA dumpster accepts furniture
- Wait until eviction morning with no booked path
