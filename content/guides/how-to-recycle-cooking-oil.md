# How to recycle cooking oil and fat near you

Searches like “how to recycle cooking oil near me” and “fat recycling service” are the same problem: grease does not belong in the sink or storm drain. It cools into fatbergs, clogs laterals, and creates expensive sewer backups.

## Household amounts (turkey fryers, deep fryers, pan grease)

### Cool, contain, then choose a path

- Let oil cool completely
- Pour into a sealed, non-breakable container with a lid
- Keep out of reach of pets (poultry grease especially)

### Where it can go

- **City organics / grease rules** — some green-cart programs accept small amounts of cooled oil; many do not
- **Cooking-oil / grease recyclers** — restaurants use them; some accept residential jugs on posted days
- **Transfer / HHW guidance** — a few counties list cooking oil; others say sealed trash only for tiny amounts
- **Never** the storm drain or toilet

See [cooking oil](/materials/cooking-oil).

Solidified bacon grease in a scrap jar is still better in trash (where allowed) than down the pipe. Liquid volumes from a turkey fryer need a recycler or municipal instruction — not the garden soil.

## “Fat recycling service” for restaurants and heavy users

Commercial kitchens contract grease trap and used-oil services. If you searched that phrase from a food-business context:

- Use a licensed renderer / used-cooking-oil hauler
- Keep outdoor oil tanks locked — theft and spills are common
- Do not pour fryer oil into a dumpster

Home users rarely need a commercial “fat recycling service”; a municipal drop-off or a recycler that accepts residential containers is enough.

## What not to do

- Pour oil down the kitchen sink “with hot water and soap”
- Dump fryer oil in a creek or alley
- Mix oil with paint or solvents
- Store open grease where rodents can reach it
