# How to dispose of fluorescent bulbs and CFLs

Compact fluorescent lamps (CFLs) and tube fluorescents contain mercury. The amount in one bulb is small, but trash trucks and landfills are the wrong place for mercury. Most U.S. cities and retailers therefore steer residents to **HHW** or **retail take-back**, not the blue cart.

LED bulbs are a different product — see [LED bulbs](/materials/led-bulbs). Incandescent bulbs are usually trash — see [incandescent bulbs](/materials/incandescent-bulbs). This guide is about mercury-containing lamps.

## Why fluorescents are special

Mercury vapor is released when a lamp breaks. Programs exist to:

- Keep mercury out of landfill leachate
- Protect workers who compact trash
- Capture phosphor and glass through lamp recyclers

Wishcycling a tube into mixed recycling often means the lamp breaks on the sorting line.

## Best drop-off options

### Retail take-back

Many hardware and home-improvement stores host CFL bins near the entrance. Tube acceptance varies — four-foot tubes may need HHW instead. Call the store; bin programs change.

### Household hazardous waste

County HHW facilities commonly list fluorescent lamps and CFLs. Bring lamps intact in a box. Some sites also take ballasts; ask whether PCB ballasts from old fixtures have separate rules.

### Municipal e-waste or lamp events

A few cities collect lamps on e-waste days. Do not assume every electronics event wants four-foot tubes — check the accepted list.

## Storing bulbs until you can drop them off

- Keep CFLs in their original sleeves or a small box so they cannot rattle loose
- Store tubes horizontally in a rigid container
- Do not tape a pile of bare tubes together in a way that stresses the glass
- Keep storage away from kids’ play areas

## If a bulb breaks

Treat it as a small mercury incident, not a normal sweep-up:

- Air the room; keep people and pets out briefly
- Do not vacuum first — vacuuming can spread mercury vapor and powder
- Scoop glass and powder with stiff paper or cardboard onto stiff paper; wipe with damp paper towels
- Put debris in a sealed container or jar for HHW if your local guidance says so
- Check EPA or your city health department’s current broken-CFL steps for details that match your area

Commercial spills and many broken tubes are above DIY scope — call local guidance.

## Ballasts, fixtures, and retrofit kits

Old magnetic ballasts may contain PCBs. Do not smash them open. Ask HHW or a lamp recycler how they want ballasts packaged. Empty aluminum fixtures without lamps often go as scrap metal or bulky metal — remove the lamps first.

## What not to do

- Toss CFLs in the toilet or crush them in a bag “to save space”
- Put long tubes in the blue bin beside cardboard
- Burn lamp waste
- Leave a box of tubes at a closed HHW gate after hours
- Mix broken-lamp debris into regular trash if your city asks for HHW intake
